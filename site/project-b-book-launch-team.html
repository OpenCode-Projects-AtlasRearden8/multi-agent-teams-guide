Project B - Book-Launch Team

One nonfiction book, one calm launch week, zero invented selling points.

The project brief (fictional but realistic)

You are a beginner-friendly nonfiction author with a book about doing your own simple household budgeting — we will use the fictional title Calm Money by "J. Reader" — going live as a self-published paperback and ebook next month. You need launch-week materials: six social posts, one web page blurb, and one email for your (very small) newsletter list.

Why a team helps: every one of your launch claims must trace back to what the book actually offers. A lone writing agent (trained on lots of other people's puffery) will happily promise "instant results," "a complete system," or "the only method you'll ever need." Those sentences cost trust with your first two hundred readers — and with platform reviewers.

Education note: nothing here is marketing, publishing, or legal advice; follow your platform's current publishing policies and be skeptical of anyone's claims, including ours.

The roster

Role Owns Produces
Boss (you) kickoff.md, final/ approvals, the book facts
Librarian facts/ book-fact-sheet.md + reader-profile.md
Writer drafts/ 6 posts, 1 blurb, 1 email
Checker (claims) notes/ claim-by-claim review memos
Checker (tone) notes/ one plain-English voice memo

Four agents again — librarian, writer, and two checkers. The claims checker verifies every factual sentence against facts/book-fact-sheet.md. The tone checker reads with one question: "does this sound like a friend explaining, never condescending?" Two narrow checks beat one broad one. (The kickoff in Step 1 below can come from a five-minute planner cameo, or you can just write it yourself.)

The ground-truth files (librarian, BEFORE anything launches)

facts/book-fact-sheet.md (abbreviated sample):

STATUS: DRAFT — boss-owned facts, transcribed by librarian
BOOK: Calm Money by J. Reader — paperback + ebook, Feb 2026 (fictional)
WHAT IT IS: a 140-page plain-English guide; 12 small budgeting habits
(one small habit a week); 6 printable worksheets; worked example budget
WHAT IT IS NOT: not investing advice; no get-rich promises; no apps to install
WORKS FOR: people new to a budget; ADHD-friendly "one habit a week" pace
PAGE COUNT: 140 (125 body + TOC + worksheets)
FORMATS: paperback; ebook (two formats total — no audiobook)
SERIES: first book; a companion journal is *not* announced yet
CLAIMS BANNED UNTIL TRUE: "instant savings," "no budgeting experience
required to finish in an hour," "guaranteed results," "endorsed by an expert"

That last section — claims banned until true — is the librarian's best invention. It lists, in plain sight, the exact phrases the writer must not use. The checker's job becomes mechanical.

facts/reader-profile.md (abbreviated):

WHO READS THIS: adults new-ish to budgeting; often busy, often tired
NEEDS: small wins, no shame, weekly tempo
DOES NOT WANT: lectures, jargon, "you should already know this" tone
WHERE THEY ARE: one email list (~230 volunteers), a small web page,
and 2 social accounts you actually enjoy using

Step 1 — Kickoff (you + planner, one short chat)

Goal: run a calm launch week for the book "Calm Money" (self-published).
Definition of done: 6 posts + 1 page blurb + 1 email in final/, every claim
traceable to facts/book-fact-sheet.md, tone passes the friend test, boss OK.
Roster: BOSS; LIBRARIAN (facts/); WRITER (drafts/); CHECKER-CLAIMS (notes/);
CHECKER-TONE (notes/). One writer per file. Unknown facts: never invented.
Time-box: launch week, 7 days, 3 passes max.

Step 2 — Writing pass (writer)

The librarian hands off; the writer produces drafts in drafts/, one file per deliverable, each ending with a claim map like Project A. Abbreviated sample, drafts/post-03-DRAFT.md:

STATUS: DRAFT (writer)
POST 3 (short, social):
Budgeting wonky? Same.
That's why Calm Money (out Feb) has 12 tiny habits — one a week,
worksheets included, nothing to install.
This week's tiny habit: write down money-in before you plan money-out.
You can do this one in 10 minutes. (It's literally page 113.)
CLAIM MAP: 12 tiny habits -> fact-sheet [WHAT IT IS]; one a week,
ADHD-friendly pace -> fact-sheet, reader-profile [WORKS FOR];
worksheets -> fact-sheet [6 worksheets]; page 113 -> BANNED-PENDING
(habit 9 is p.61 in proofs; NEEDS LIBRARIAN); nothing to install ->
fact-sheet [WHAT IT IS NOT]

Note the writer flagging its own claim (BANNED-PENDING ... NEEDS LIBRARIAN). Good habit: whenever you hand an agent the claim-map instruction, tell it "if you cannot source a claim, leave the claim out or flag it — do not paper over it."

Step 3 — The two checks

Claims checker memo (notes/review-post-03-claims.md, abbreviated):

STATUS: BOUNCE (1 item)
- "It's literally page 113" -> page unverified (flagged by writer).
  Fact sheet says TOC is being re-checked; do not cite page numbers until
  facts/ confirms pages. Suggest: remove page cite, keep habit framing.
PASS: 12 habits, 6 worksheets, no promise language, formats correct.

Tone checker memo (notes/review-post-03-tone.md, abbreviated):

STATUS: PASS with 1 nit
- "You can do this one in 10 minutes" — a time promise the fact sheet
  doesn't make. Not a claims violation, but a time-promise nit: soften to
  "a quick one, good for tired evenings."
Overall voice: warm, zero condescension. Passes the friend test.

Two memos, neither one rewrites the post. The writer fixes and resends; both memos read APPROVED on round 2, and the post moves to final/.

Step 4 — Launch week itself is you

The team's job ended at approved files. You paste posts when you choose to. The email you send. Prices and dates you set. Keep final/ as your launch binder; when something succeeds (or flops), log a one-line entry into notes/decisions.md — future-you (and future launches) will want the pattern.

Adapt this project

  • Any small-business launch (a zine, a course, a service page) reuses this shape: facts file → banned-claims list → drafts with claim maps → two narrow checkers.
  • Healthcare-adjacent books: if your nonfiction touches health topics, keep the same discipline and add a standing rule — the claims checker also verifies nothing implies medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment, and that your "education only, not medical advice" disclaimer appears where your platform requires it.
  • Small lists are fine. 230 volunteer readers is a real audience. The tone checker exists precisely so early readers feel like humans you are explaining to, not metrics.

Where the return was, in one sentence

The banned-claims list turned puffery-policing into a five-minute file, claim maps turned every review into a word-by-word scavenger hunt — and two false promises ("instant results" in a draft blurb, a phantom "one-hour" claim) died in the drafts folder instead of on your homepage.
